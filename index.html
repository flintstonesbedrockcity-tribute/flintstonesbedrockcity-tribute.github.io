KELOWNA, BC  ·  1968 – 1998

Flintstones
Bedrock City

Thirty years of Stone Age family fun on Highway 97 North at McCurdy Road — the place where a generation of Okanagan kids met Fred, rode the Lava Creek Waterway, and posed beside a 40-foot Fred Flintstone.

01The Park

Flintstones Bedrock City was a licensed Hanna-Barbera theme park in Kelowna, British Columbia — a full-size, walk-through version of the cartoon town of Bedrock, built for families driving the Okanagan highway. It opened in 1968 and ran for thirty years, closing in 1998.

In its 1973 advertising it billed itself as “Canada’s only Flintstones-themed amusement park” — true at the time, since the sister park near Chilliwack wouldn’t open until 1975. Daily hours were 9 a.m. to dusk, and the directions in the ads read like a time capsule: “HIGHWAY 97N at McCURDY RD (next turn north of Drive-In Theatre) at Carlton Mobile Homes.”

Who built it

The park was built by Bob Ell. The Ell family later built a second Bedrock City near Bridal Falls in 1975 — the park that would become Dinotown — with Bob’s son Rob Ell running it through the Dinotown years to its 2010 closure. The two BC parks were their own Ell-family operation, separate from the American Bedrock City parks in South Dakota and Arizona.

The park sat right on the highway, and its towering Fred Flintstone sign was a landmark for northbound drivers for decades — a 1990s photo taken from a car on Highway 97 shows the 40-foot Fred still standing by McCurdy Road, with the Wild Waters water-slide complex beside it.

02The Attractions

The 1973 ad promised “ACRES and ACRES OF FAMILY FUN,” and the fine print reads like a checklist of everything a kid in the seventies could want:

  • Walk-through cartoon environment — the town of Bedrock rebuilt in full size, with stone-age homes to explore
  • Replica Flintmobile cars — Fred’s foot-powered log car, ready for photos
  • Fountains, slides, volcanoes and waterfalls — the prehistoric landscape, built for climbing and cooling off
  • Free log-boat rides on the Lava Creek Waterway — the park’s own little river ride
  • 18-hole mini-golf — a full miniature course on site
  • Nightly outdoor movies at the Rockmore Theatre — films under the Okanagan sky
  • “Bedrock Radio” station — the park’s own station
  • Air-conditioned gift shop — souvenirs to take home
  • Shaded picnic tables and two food stands — fuel for the family

Later years added their own landmarks: a giant purple Dino statue that kids posed with through the eighties, costumed characters roaming the grounds, and the water slides of the neighbouring Wild Waters complex going up next door in 1980.

Why it closed

The end wasn’t about crowds — it was about paperwork. Through the 1980s and ’90s the licensing for Hanna-Barbera’s characters changed hands again and again before landing with Time Warner, and when the Kelowna park’s license came up in 1998 it wasn’t renewed. The Chilliwack park survived by dropping the Flintstones theme and continuing as Dinotown; Kelowna’s park simply closed, and the buildings came down.

04Where Is Fred Now?

Every comment thread about Bedrock City eventually asks the same question: what happened to the giant Fred? Local legend says someone hauled the massive Fred statue away and it’s lying in a field somewhere out by the Kelowna airport. Nobody’s produced a photo — but nobody’s disproven it either, and the rumour has outlived the park by nearly thirty years.

And Fred was a landmark in more ways than one: the founder reportedly classified the 40-foot plywood Fred as a “lawn ornament” to get around signage restrictions — per a founder’s-family interview cited by GoKanagan.

“Rumour has it that someone took the massive Fred statue and it’s lying in a crop somewhere by the Kelowna airport.” — from the description of 1980 home-movie footage of the park

05Souvenirs

The park is gone, but its paper trail survives. Bedrock City Kelowna memorabilia still turns up in collections and online auctions:

  • Postcards — including a 1970s card showing kids posing with costumed Dino, Yogi Bear and a hippo against the park’s rock walls, plus attraction cards for the Bedrock Express train, the Bedrock Taxi and Dino
  • Pennants — felt “Bedrock City, Kelowna B.C.” banners from 1973, 1975 and 1978
  • Tableware — salt & pepper shaker sets, plates, “Cactus Juice” glasses, frosted 1973 glasses, milk-glass mugs, a wooden serving bowl, a 1974 ashtray and “The Bedrock Bowl” (1986)
  • Apparel — a “Staff of ’92” t-shirt, a 1997 park t-shirt and a mesh trucker cap
  • Ephemera — a 1973 plastic shopping bag, a complete 1976 playing-card deck, a souvenir spoon, “Yabba Dabba Doo” buttons
  • Barware — a “Yabba Dabba Doo” bottle opener stamped Kelowna B.C., © Hanna-Barbera 1978
  • Patches — a “Flintstones Bedrock City – Kelowna B.C.” souvenir patch
  • Bumper stickers — vintage Bedrock City and Wild Waters stickers
